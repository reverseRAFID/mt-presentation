# 01 — Slides

Nine slides. `SPOKEN` is the script as delivered — word counts are tuned to the timing budget. `ON SLIDE` is everything the audience reads. `NOTES` is for the presenter and the builder.

Running total is cumulative. If you are behind at slide 6, cut the second half of slide 8, not slide 5.

---

## Slide 1 — Utah becomes Mars
**0:00–0:20 · 40 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- Rover: Taurus (2026)
- One line: *Built in Dhaka. Tested on Mars.*

**SPOKEN**
> Every May, a stretch of desert in Utah becomes Mars. Thirty-eight university teams from around the world bring rovers there. This year, one of them was built in Dhaka — by undergraduates, in a lab at BRAC University.

**NOTES**
Open on the machine, not the team name. The title of the talk can live in the corner. Do not put "BRACU Mongol-Tori" in 100pt type — the audience gets the name on slide 3 and it lands harder after the hook.

## Slide 2 — What the University Rover Challenge is
**0:20–0:55 · 75 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- The Mars Society (U.S. non-profit) · annual since 2007
- Mars Desert Research Station, Hanksville, Utah
- 2026: **116 teams · 18 countries → 38 finalists**
- Four missions: Science · Delivery · Equipment Servicing · Autonomous Navigation

**SPOKEN**
> The University Rover Challenge is run by The Mars Society, an American non-profit. It has been held since 2007 at the Mars Desert Research Station in Utah, and it is the biggest student robotics competition in the world. This year a hundred and sixteen teams from eighteen countries entered. Thirty-eight made the finals. Over three days, every rover must run field science, deliver cargo, service equipment, and drive itself — with no line of sight to its operators.

**NOTES**
This is the U.S. anchor of the whole talk and the reason it belongs at this event. Say "American non-profit" out loud. The 116 → 38 funnel is the most legible way to convey difficulty to a non-technical audience.

## Slide 3 — Who we are
**0:55–1:25 · 65 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- BRACU Mongol-Tori — মঙ্গল তরী, "Mars vessel"
- ~60 undergraduates · 6 sub-teams
- Mechanical · Electronics · Controls & Software · Autonomous · Network & Vision · Astrobiology
- Eight rovers since 2018

**SPOKEN**
> We are BRACU Mongol-Tori. Mongol Tori means Mars vessel. We are a student team at BRAC University — about sixty undergraduates across six sub-teams: mechanical, electronics, controls, autonomous, network and vision, and astrobiology. Everything on the rover is designed and built in-house. We have built eight rovers since 2018. This year's is called Taurus.

**NOTES**
The Bangla name is worth two seconds. It is the first signal that this is not an imported project.

## Slide 4 — What the machine actually has to do
**1:25–2:05 · 85 words**

**ON SLIDE**
Four panels, one per mission, each with the subsystem that answers it:
- **Equipment Servicing** → 7-DOF arm · lifts >5 kg · grips objects >7.5 cm
- **Delivery** → four-wheel rocker-bogie · 50 kg · 120×120×120 cm
- **Science** → onboard soil assay, biosignature detection
- **Autonomous Navigation** → ROS 2 Humble · differential GNSS RTK · ZED 2i stereo vision

**SPOKEN**
> Here is what that machine has to do. Fifty kilograms. A seven-degree-of-freedom arm that can lift five kilos and flip a switch. A rocker-bogie suspension, the same principle NASA uses, so all four wheels stay on the rock. An onboard laboratory that takes soil, runs assays, and looks for signs of past life. And autonomy — ROS 2, RTK GPS, stereo vision — that drives it to a marker hundreds of metres away with nobody touching the controls.

**NOTES**
The densest slide in the deck. It earns the right to the failure story that follows. Say the jargon at speed and do not explain it — the point is that the list is long, not that the audience parses every item. Anyone who wants detail will come to the table afterwards.

## Slide 5 — The part that is not on the spec sheet
**2:05–2:45 · 85 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- **2023 · Utah** — circuit burned out mid-competition. Rebuilt on site in **4 hours**.
- **2024 · Utah** — chassis broke during the autonomous run. Fixed in **6 hours**.
- Commercial 6-axis arm: **BDT 5,00,000+**. Ours, from recycled brushed DC motors: **under BDT 20,000**.

**SPOKEN**
> Now the part that is not on the spec sheet. In 2023, our circuit burned out in the middle of the Utah desert. The team rebuilt it in four hours, on site, with whatever they had. In 2024, the chassis broke during an autonomous run. Six hours to fix. We finished twenty-first that year. And because a commercial six-axis arm costs over five lakh taka, the team built theirs from recycled brushed DC motors for under twenty thousand.

**NOTES**
The emotional centre of the talk. Slow down here. Do not apologise for the failures and do not add a redemptive clause — slide 6 does that work. The BDT contrast is the line students will remember and repeat.

## Slide 6 — Every season since 2018
**2:45–3:20 · 70 words**

**ON SLIDE**
Rank chart, lower is better, 2024 marked as the low point:

| Year | Global rank |
|---|---|
| 2018 | 13th |
| 2019 | 11th |
| 2020 | 3rd (design review only — finals cancelled, COVID) |
| 2021 | Finals cancelled, COVID |
| 2022 | 16th |
| 2023 | 16th |
| 2024 | 21st |
| 2025 | 8th |
| 2026 | **7th** |

**SPOKEN**
> This is every season since 2018. Thirteenth. Eleventh. Third on the design review the year the finals were cancelled. Sixteenth. Sixteenth. Then twenty-first — the year everything broke. Then eighth. Then seventh. The line that matters is the one after the failure. Nothing on this chart got better because a season went smoothly.

**NOTES**
Be precise about 2020 and 2021 out loud — the URC finals were cancelled both years, and there will be people in the room who know that. Claiming an unqualified "3rd place in 2020" is the one thing in this deck that could cost credibility.

## Slide 7 — URC 2026
**3:20–3:50 · 65 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- **7th of 116** — top 6%
- Science mission: **95 / 100**
- Equipment Servicing: 3rd-highest score of any team
- Delivery: 4th-highest score of any team
- Bangladesh in the 2026 finals: **5 teams**. UIU Mars Rover finished **3rd overall.**

**SPOKEN**
> This May we finished seventh in the world. Ninety-five out of a hundred on the science mission. Third-highest score of any team on equipment servicing. And we were not alone — five Bangladeshi teams reached the finals, and United International University finished third overall, on the podium with Missouri and Monash.

**NOTES**
Ending on UIU rather than on ourselves is deliberate and it is the right call for this room. It converts a team result into a national one, which is the story the Embassy and EMK actually care about.

## Slide 8 — The competition is the deadline, not the point
**3:50–4:30 · 85 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- **SATEL** (Finland) — verified **3.3 km** ground teleoperation range, tested in Bangladeshi conditions
- **CompleTech** (Finland) — custom high-gain antenna from our RF propagation mapping at Potenga, Chattogram. They are now building a commercial antenna-tracking module **on our codebase.**
- **SBG Systems** (France) — tactical-grade inertial navigation
- **myActuator** — precision manipulator arm programme
- **16+ schools and colleges** mentored in Dhaka and Chattogram
- Title sponsor: **Meghna Group of Industries**

**SPOKEN**
> The competition is the deadline, not the point. To get a reliable radio link we worked with SATEL in Finland and verified three point three kilometres of control range in Bangladeshi conditions. We mapped radio propagation along the marine drive at Potenga to design a custom antenna with CompleTech — who are now building a commercial product on our codebase. And over the past year the team has run robotics sessions in more than sixteen schools and colleges in Dhaka and Chattogram.

**NOTES**
The CompleTech line is the strongest single sentence available: student work from Dhaka flowing back into a European company's product. If time is short, cut SBG and myActuator from the spoken script but leave them on the slide.

**CUT POINT** — if running long, drop the outreach sentence here and fold "sixteen schools" into slide 9.

## Slide 9 — Close
**4:30–5:00 · 60 words**

**ON SLIDE**
- Close-up: jamdani motifs etched into the CNC-cut aluminium shell
- *We recruit every semester.*
- QR → join page

**SPOKEN**
> One last thing. Etched into the aluminium shell of our rover are jamdani motifs. If that machine ever reaches Mars, something Bangladeshi goes with it. You do not need a space agency to start. You need a team, a deadline, and somewhere to break things. Come find us — we recruit every semester. Thank you.

**NOTES**
The jamdani close is the deck's best asset and the reason it is held to the end. It ties the engineering back to the culture, in a room whose entire purpose is cultural exchange. Make sure the photograph is a real macro shot of the etched shell — a jamdani textile stock photo would undo the point.

Hold this slide up during Q&A. The QR code is the only conversion mechanism in the talk.
