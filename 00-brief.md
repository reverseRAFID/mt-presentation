# 00 — Brief

## The event

A STEM fair hosted by the **Edward M. Kennedy (EMK) Center** with the **U.S. Embassy Dhaka**. The EMK Center is the Embassy's flagship "American Space" in Bangladesh, running since September 2012, operated jointly with the JAAGO Foundation. It houses a MakerLab, an AR/VR lab, and a full EducationUSA advising centre, and sits inside a network of 700+ American Spaces in 140 countries.

This context should shape the deck, not just decorate it. Three things follow from it:

- **The host's mission is youth STEM access and U.S.–Bangladesh people-to-people ties.** Our strongest card is not the ranking — it's that the whole story runs through an American competition, an American desert, and now feeds back into Bangladeshi classrooms.
- **The audience is mixed.** School and college students, university students, EducationUSA advisers, Embassy staff, other exhibitor teams, possibly press. Pitch the technical content so a Class 10 student follows the shape and an engineer respects the substance.
- **Some people in the room will know URC.** Do not overclaim. Every number on a slide must survive someone Googling it mid-talk. See `02-facts.md`.

## The goal

One sentence: **make a room of Bangladeshi students believe that a world-class engineering result is reachable from a lab in Dhaka, and show them the door.**

Secondary goals, in priority order:

1. Recruitment — students should leave knowing Mongol-Tori takes new members every semester.
2. Credibility with the Embassy/EMK for future collaboration (MakerLab access, outreach partnership, EducationUSA pathway stories).
3. Sponsor and partner visibility.

Not a goal: winning an argument about who is Bangladesh's best rover team. Being generous about UIU's 3rd-place finish makes our story bigger, not smaller.

## Format constraints

- **5 minutes hard.** Fairs run to schedule and the next presenter is standing there.
- **9 slides.** ~33 seconds each. Word budget ≈ 630 spoken words at ~130 wpm, which lands at about 4:50 with buffer.
- Assume a **projector in a lit room** with an audience partly standing. Large type, high contrast, no thin hairlines, no small-print charts.
- Assume **no reliable click-through of video**. If a video is used it must be a short muted loop that carries meaning with the sound off.
- Assume the presenter may be handed a clicker with only arrow keys.

## Narrative spine

The deck is built on one reversal, and everything serves it:

> A team from a country with no space agency competes in America's premier student rover competition. It gets good, then it fails badly, then it comes back better than before. The comeback is the story, not the ranking.

The 2024 twenty-first-place finish is the hinge. Most teams would bury it. Putting it on screen is what makes the 7th place mean something, and it is the single most useful thing this audience can take away.

## Tone

Plain, confident, specific. No "we are proud to present." No adjectives doing work that numbers can do. The presenter is an undergraduate who built the thing, not a spokesperson.
